What most people type

a song about my grandma

“Grandma's Song”

You always kept the porch light on Waiting up for me to come home With cookies warm and open arms A little laugh, your favorite song

It is not bad writing. It is just not about anyone. Porch light, cookies, open arms — every line could belong to any grandmother who ever lived.

What the book teaches you to type

Singer-songwriter. My grandma Rosa taught me to make her tortillas every Sunday in her kitchen in Tucson. She always said “use your hands, not the spoon.” She can't stand at the counter as long as she used to, so I bought her a wooden stool to sit on while she watches me try. I want the chorus to land on her saying that line.

“Use Your Hands”

Sunday morning, Tucson sun Grandma Rosa in the kitchen, flour on her thumbs Apron tied, she smiles at me “Mi niña, let's make memories”

The title came out of the detail, not the topic. Tucson, the flour, the apron, the stool — nothing was wasted. And the chorus landed on her line, exactly where it was asked to.
