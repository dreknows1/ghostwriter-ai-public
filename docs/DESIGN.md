# DESIGN — direction decision

Three directions were built as standalone mockups and rendered at 1440px
(`docs/variants/`). The call, and the reasoning, below.

## What each one proved

**A — Editorial Cream.** The strongest system by a distance. Enormous Unbounded H1
on generous cream, asymmetric two-column grids, hairline rules, numbered items,
small-caps eyebrows. Confident and unmistakably art-directed; nothing about it reads
as a template. Its one real failure: it isn't cinematic. Rudy sat in a small inset
frame, which would waste the footage we generated.

**B — Midnight Booth.** The execution failed but the premise didn't. With only a CSS
placeholder to work with, the hero became a flat grey wedge on near-black under a
glowing centred headline — precisely the generic dark-AI-startup hero the research
memo warns about. What it did prove is that the *dark interlude* is worth having:
the real Higgsfield footage is a lit studio, and it needs full-bleed room to land.

**C — The Writing Desk.** Twee in the wrong places (a floating card on a dull grey
field, not cream) but it invented three details worth keeping: a struck-through
lyric line, a `[Chorus]` annotation pencilled in the margin, and the prompt line
being typed live in accent blue.

## The decision

**Direction A is the chassis.** Its typographic system, grid, rules and restraint
carry the whole page. Onto it:

1. **The hero goes full-bleed cinematic.** Not a small inset. Cream type block on the
   left, the real Rudy video bleeding off the right edge at full height. Editorial
   composition, cinema-scale imagery — this is what fixes A's only weakness.
2. **Two dark interludes** (B's premise, executed with real footage rather than CSS):
   "Meet Rudy" and the closing CTA. Each is a story beat that earns going dark; the
   page is not a dark/light stripe pattern.
3. **C's artifact details** move into "It starts with something you can't say", where
   a struck-through line, a margin `[Chorus]`, and one live-typed prompt are about the
   craft rather than decoration.
4. **A's "how it works" and features systems stay**, filled with real captured footage.

A's invented H2 for the features section — *"Everything a session writer would
bring"* — is better than the brief's placeholder and is approved into the copy deck.

## Rejected, and why

- **Centred hero** (B): centring the headline throws away the asymmetry that makes A
  look designed rather than generated.
- **Headline glow / bloom** (B): reads cheap at any size. Contrast comes from real
  lighting in the footage, not from a text-shadow.
- **Card-on-a-field hero** (C): a floating rounded card is the single most template-ish
  shape available. The paper idea survives as texture and annotation, not as a card.
- **Social proof band** (research memo): we have no verified rating or reviews.
  Fabricating them is off the table.
- **Scroll-scrubbed hero video** (research memo): the hero is a mood loop, not a
  product scrub. Scrubbing buys nothing and is the memo's own top mobile-jank risk.
